# Decision — H2 killed; ship the simpler retention ranking

- **Date:** 2026-10-01
- **Evidence:** [../results/h2-credit-driven-retention.md](../results/h2-credit-driven-retention.md)
- **Decision: do not ship credit-driven retention.** Default to naive usage-success.

## What was decided

H2 was **killed** under the pre-registered rule. Ranking retention by estimated causal credit beat retrieval-frequency (+0.126) and recency+importance (+0.090) but lost to a smoothed success rate (−0.012, interval straddling zero).

The oracle reaches only 0.485 against random's 0.352, and naive captures 93% of that gain. There was almost nothing for a better estimator to win.

## Why, in one line

**Rank correlation was the wrong proxy.** H1 measured the whole ordering (ρ=0.76 vs 0.47); retention needs one threshold to be right. Harm is directly visible in raw outcomes — a bad memory that gets followed produces failures — so the debiasing that wins on ρ buys little at the bottom of the ranking.

## What changes

1. **`RetentionPolicy` should default to naive usage-success**, not the amortized estimator. It is simpler, needs no propensity logging, and is statistically indistinguishable from the better estimator here.
2. **Do not market retention-by-causal-credit.** No evidence supports it.
3. **The amortized estimator stays.** H1 stands; it is the right choice where the full ordering matters, and it preserved rare-but-correct memories far better than naive (87.0% vs 61.1%) — a difference invisible in mean success precisely because rare events are rare.
4. **`frequency` ranking is disqualified.** Worse than random, and it keeps 9.3% of rare-but-correct memories. Nothing should fall back to "keep what gets retrieved most".

## What does not change

P6 (federation, H3) proceeds. Sharing is a different decision: the cost of sending a harmful memory to another agent is higher than keeping it locally, and the full ordering matters more than a single threshold — which is the regime where the amortized estimator's advantage actually lives.

## Next: pre-register H2b at a tight budget

The exploratory sweep in the result's appendix (post-hoc, 5 seeds, **not** registered) found the ordering reverses as the budget tightens: amortized beats naive by **+0.060** at budget 20 and by +0.032 at budget 10, with intervals clear of zero, against +0.006 at the registered budget of 40.

That reconciles H1 and H2. The estimator's advantage lives in the middle and top of the ranking — telling a *valuable* memory from a merely *harmless* one. A loose budget never asks that question, because there is room to keep both; it only asks "drop the harmful", which raw outcomes answer directly. A tight budget asks it on every slot.

**This does not change the decision.** The budget was chosen after seeing which one won, on 5 seeds. The disciplined step is a fresh pre-registration:

> **H2b** — at a budget that binds (20 of ~120), credit-ranked retention beats naive usage-success by ≥ 5pp, with fresh seeds and the threshold fixed in advance.

Until H2b runs, naive remains the default.

## Other conditions worth testing

- A domain where harm is **not** directly observable in the outcome (delayed or diffuse failures), removing naive's main advantage.
- Credit-driven **writing** rather than only forgetting, which this design never tested.

# Decision — P9: do not build distributed infrastructure

- **Date:** 2026-10-07
- **Decision: declined, on measurement.** The gate is not met and is not close.

## The gate

ROADMAP P9: *"A load one machine demonstrably cannot carry."*

## The measurement

| memories | episodes | events | retrieve latency | store size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 80 | 2,000 | 11,140 | 2.65 ms | 3.6 MB |
| 400 | 5,000 | 35,935 | 2.98 ms | 13.0 MB |
| 2,000 | 10,000 | 120,089 | **4.39 ms** | 47.9 MB |

**25× more memories costs 1.7× latency.** Retrieval scales sub-linearly, because a query only scores memories that share signal with it — the relevance floor and sparse-query path cut the rest before the comparison.

At 4.39 ms a single thread serves roughly **230 retrievals per second**, on one laptop, in pure Python, with no index beyond a flat scan. Storage extrapolates to ~400 MB per million events.

## Consequence

Nothing in this project has produced a load that one machine struggles with, and nothing on the roadmap predicts one. Building sharding, gossip, partition handling and Byzantine defences now would be building for a problem that does not exist — the exact failure AGENTS.md principle 9 exists to prevent, and the one CRITICAL_REVIEW §6 warned about when it put the entire distributed-systems layer (FSDP, ZeRO, gossip, low-bit communication, P2P) off the critical path.

**P9 is closed as declined, not deferred.** The difference matters: deferred implies it is coming. This says the evidence points the other way, and re-opening it requires evidence, not a change of mood.

## What would re-open it

A measured load, not an anticipated one. Concretely, any of:

- **Latency** above ~50 ms per retrieval at the deployment's real store size, after the cheap fixes are exhausted — a real embedder with an ANN index, and Postgres instead of SQLite, both of which are already seams in the architecture and neither of which has been needed yet.
- **Write contention** from genuinely concurrent agents. The current store is single-process; the first real multi-writer deployment is the thing to measure, and the answer may be Postgres rather than distribution.
- **A trust boundary**, not a throughput one. Separate organisations federating is a governance requirement that happens to need separate stores. `federation.py` already crosses store boundaries, so this is the most likely genuine driver — and it would call for a federation protocol, not sharding.

Note that the third is the only one the research actually points toward, and it is the one that needs the least distributed machinery.

## What was delivered instead

The measurement above, this record, and the observation that the two cheap scaling levers (real embedder + ANN, Postgres) remain untouched because nothing has needed them.

# R1 — the relevance benchmark and its oracle ceiling

**The headroom is real.** Perfect selection beats the best simple baseline by
**+0.222 nDCG@3 on dev** and **+0.196 on test**, and drives critical-memory
omission from 20–40% to zero. That contradicts the prediction I registered in
[RELEVANCE_SPEC.md](../../docs/RELEVANCE_SPEC.md) §2 before running it, and the
contradiction is the most useful thing in this document.

- **Benchmark:** `relevance_v1`, 20 cases over 15 scenario families, 18–20 memories per corpus, `k=3`
- **Built:** 2026-10-11, **before any ranker existed**; the commit history shows it
- **Splits:** `dev` 15 cases (tunable), `test` 5 cases (**frozen, untouched during this work**)
- **Run:** `python -m experiments.relevance_bench --split {dev,test}` — deterministic, no model calls, under a second
- **Raw:** [dev](relevance-v1-dev.md) · [test](relevance-v1-test.md)

---

## 1. The numbers

### dev (15 cases)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **`lexical`** | **0.778** | 0.452 | 0.821 | 0.714 | 0.286 | 0.000 | 0.429 |

The ceiling held on both splits: `stats.check_ceiling` reports *no arm beat

## 2. The prediction was wrong, and here is why that matters

[RELEVANCE_SPEC.md](../../docs/RELEVANCE_SPEC.md) §2 recorded this before the run:

> Perfect *selection* over the existing corpus was worth about **+5 points**;
> the rest of the gap was corpus **coverage**. […] If the new benchmark's
> oracle ceiling is also ~5 points, relevance ranking is not where the headroom
> is.

It is not ~5 points. It is **+0.222 nDCG and a 28.6-point reduction in critical
omission**. Two honest readings, and both belong here:

**The reading that favours the work.** Phase A's corpus was *generated by the
agents themselves* during the run, so its coverage was whatever the episodes
happened to produce, and the selection problem was correspondingly easy — three
candidates, one right answer. `relevance_v1` has 18–20 memories per task with
deliberate lexical traps, near-duplicates and distractors. On a realistic
corpus, selection has room.

**The reading that restrains it.** These are *retrieval* metrics, not task
success. Phase A's +5 points was measured in verified outcomes through a real
agent; +0.222 nDCG is not convertible into that, and the conversion is exactly
what R6 has to measure rather than assume. A benchmark I designed can also be
harder than the world: I wrote the distractors, and I chose `k=3`.

So the claim this licenses is narrow: **on a corpus with realistic distractor
density, selection has measurable headroom.** It does not license "relevance
ranking improves agent outcomes". That needs R6.

## 3. The shipping retrieval path is the weakest real arm

`current` — `Memory Fabric.retrieve()` exactly as it ships — scores **0.728 on
dev and 0.485 on test**, tying `vector` because it *is* vector retrieval over a
hashing embedder, and losing to a plain IDF-weighted token overlap by 5 points
on dev and **32 points on test**.

That is a product finding, not a research one, and it is actionable now:
**adding a lexical arm to the default retrieval path is likely worth more than
any ranker.** It is also the cheapest possible change, and R2's threshold should
be set against `lexical` rather than against `current`, or the ranker gets
credit for beating a baseline the product should not have been using.

The caveat that bounds all of this: **the vector arm is a hashing embedder, not
a trained model.** It is the single biggest limitation of every number above. A
real embedding model would likely move `vector`, `hybrid` and `current`
together, and might close most of the headroom on its own — which would be a
better outcome than a ranker and should be tested first.

## 3a. Acted on: hybrid retrieval is now the default

Measured before and after on both splits, with `store_vector` pinning the old
behaviour so the comparison stays checkable:

| arm | dev nDCG | dev critical omission | test nDCG | test critical omission |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `store_vector` (the old default) | 0.728 | 0.286 | 0.485 | 0.400 |
| **`current` (hybrid, the new default)** | **0.752** | **0.214** | **0.658** | **0.000** |
| `store_lexical` | 0.756 | 0.286 | 0.804 | 0.200 |

On the frozen test split the product arm gains **+0.173 nDCG** and critical
omission goes from **40% to zero**.

**The default is a trade-off, not a win on every axis.** Lexical alone still
has the better nDCG — 0.804 against 0.658 on test — while hybrid has the better
critical-memory omission and recall. Critical omission is the metric with a
consequence: it counts the cases where the one decisive memory never reached
the agent. So the default optimises that and gives up precision, and
`retrieval="lexical"` is available with the reasoning written down so the
choice is informed rather than inherited.

RRF rather than a weighted score blend, because cosine similarity and IDF
overlap are on incomparable scales and a weight between them would be a
parameter tuned on the dev split before any ranker milestone exists.

## 3b. Acted on: the three mechanisms the store already had

R1 §4 said three of the five highest-gap families are answerable with
provenance edges, source roots and recorded time. All three are now wired into
retrieval, each measured as its own arm, with every option named explicitly so
no arm silently inherits another's mechanism.

| arm | dev nDCG | dev crit. omission | test nDCG | test crit. omission |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `store_plain` (both off) | 0.752 | 0.214 | 0.658 | 0.000 |
| `store_temporal` (`as_of` only) | 0.762 | 0.214 | 0.732 | 0.000 |
| `store_roots` (grouping only) | 0.780 | **0.143** | 0.658 | 0.000 |
| `store_lineage` (promotion only) | 0.786 | **0.143** | 0.658 | 0.000 |
| **`store_all`** | **0.795** | **0.143** | **0.732** | 0.000 |
| `lexical` (no mechanisms at all) | 0.778 | 0.286 | **0.804** | 0.200 |
| `oracle` | 1.000 | 0.000 | 1.000 | 0.000 |

**+0.043 nDCG on dev and +0.074 on test**, and critical-memory omission from
0.214 to 0.143 on dev. That closes roughly **17% of the measured dev headroom**
— real, and modest. Root grouping and lineage promotion are now on by default;
`as_of` is not, and cannot be, because defaulting it to `now` would make every
historical question unanswerable.

**The restraint that belongs next to those numbers:** on the frozen test split
`lexical` alone still scores **0.804 against `store_all`'s 0.732**. The
mechanisms improve the product's own arm on both splits, and the product arm is
still not the best arm on test. What they do buy there is critical omission —
0.000 against lexical's 0.200 — which is the failure with a consequence.

### Two implementation findings worth more than the numbers

**Lineage expansion measured exactly zero on its first implementation.** It
appended absent parents to the candidate pool, and the pool is four times `k`
and already contained the premise — ranked below the distractors. Adding
something already present changes nothing. The case needs the premise *pulled
up* beside the conclusion that depends on it, so it now raises a parent's score
to just under its child's. `indirect` went from 0.613 to **1.000**.

**Root grouping and lineage promotion were undoing each other.** With both on,
`indirect` fell back to 0.613 — because a premise and the conclusion drawn from
it share a source root, and grouping discarded exactly what promotion had
surfaced. Grouping now spares a derivation chain and collapses only true
copies, which is the distinction it always meant: four memories quoting one
report are one piece of evidence; a conclusion and its premise are two claims
and both belong in the context.

Neither would have been visible without per-mechanism arms. An all-or-nothing
"contextual retrieval" arm would have shown +0.015 and looked like weak
evidence for a good idea.

## 3c. Followed up: the embedder was the biggest lever

§3 said the hashing embedder was the single biggest limitation of every number
here, and that a real model might close most of the headroom with no new
mechanism. **It closes about 70% of it on dev** —
[full result](relevance-v1-real-embedder.md) — more than every mechanism in
§3b combined, and it matches bounded premise promotion on `indirect` with no
lineage machinery.

On the frozen split the gain is +0.045, and the two splits disagree about
whether to fuse with lexical. Twenty cases cannot settle that, which makes
**sample size the binding constraint** on this benchmark, ahead of any
mechanism and ahead of a ranker.

## 4. Where the headroom actually is

Per-family nDCG on dev, oracle minus `lexical`:

| family | gap | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| `temporal_drift` | large | no `valid_time` reasoning anywhere in the stack — the retriever cannot tell a fact that *was* true from one that is |
| `rare_critical` | large | the decisive memory is phrased as a prohibition and shares few words with the task |
| `indirect` | large | no lineage expansion: the answer is the parent of the match, and nothing walks that edge |
| `copied_evidence` | large | four memories, one root; nothing groups by `source_root` |
| `near_duplicate` | moderate | redundancy fills the budget before the rare fact arrives |
| `direct`, `lexical_trap` | small | the baselines already handle these |

Three of those five point at mechanisms the store **already has** and retrieval
does not use: provenance edges (`indirect`), source roots
(`copied_evidence`), and recorded time (`temporal_drift`, partially). That is a
more promising direction than a better scoring function, and it is what R4 and
R5 are for.

## 5. The benchmark was invalid twice

Recorded because the broken-arm check is what caught it both times, and because
three of four simulated hypotheses in this project failed the same way — the
measurement, not the mechanism.

**First build: corpus order leaked the answer.** Cases were written with the
relevant memory first, so an arm that returned the corpus in storage order
scored **0.869 nDCG against the hybrid baseline's 0.832**. A ranker tuned
against that would have been tuned against an artefact of how I typed the JSON.
Fixed by shuffling each corpus in the generator with a seed derived from the
case id, so the committed data carries no positional signal.

**Second build: `k` was as large as the corpus.** With 3–5 memories and `k=3`,
every arm returned nearly everything: recall was 1.0 by construction and
ranking could not be wrong. `broken_no_signal` still scored **0.843**. Fixed by
adding 15 shared distractors per case, bringing each corpus to 18–20.

After both fixes `broken_no_signal` scores **0.000** with a 0.976 irrelevant
ratio. `tests/test_relevance_bench.py` now asserts both properties directly —
that fewer than 35% of cases lead with a relevant memory, and that every corpus
is at least 12 memories — so neither defect can return quietly.

**A third defect, in the scoring.** Crediting gain against the full relevant
set produced an nDCG of **1.090** for a gate-bypassing arm: an impossible
number, and worse, a scoring rule that *paid* an arm for returning a memory it
must not expose. Gain is now credited for *reachable* relevance only, so
bypassing a gate can only ever cost.

## 6. What this does not establish

1. **No downstream claim.** Nothing here ran against an agent. nDCG is not task success and the conversion is R6's job.
2. **No ranker exists.** The headroom says a ranker *could* help; it says nothing about whether one will.
3. **One author, one world.** I wrote the cases, the labels and the distractors. The labelling protocol asks for an independently labelled subset with reported agreement, and that has not been done — so the labels are a single person's judgement with a stated rationale, not an adjudicated ground truth.
4. **20 cases is small.** Five per split-family at most, and the test split is 5 cases. Per-family numbers are directional, not estimates.
5. **The embedder is a stub.** Stated in §3 and worth repeating: a trained embedding model might close most of this headroom without any new mechanism.
6. **Retrieval only.** No token-budgeted assembly (R3), so `near_duplicate` is scored on ranking alone when the real remedy is diversity in assembly.

## 7. The honest summary

> On a corpus with realistic distractor density, perfect selection is worth
> **+0.222 nDCG@3** over the best simple baseline and removes **28.6 points**
> of critical-memory omission. That contradicts the ~5-point prediction carried
> over from Phase A, and the likeliest reason is that Phase A's corpus was too
> thin for selection to matter.
>
> The immediately useful finding is not about ranking at all: **the shipping
> retrieval path is the weakest non-broken arm**, beaten by IDF-weighted token
> overlap by up to 32 points, because it is a hashing embedder. Fix that
> before building a ranker.
>
> Three of the five families with the largest gaps — indirect, copied evidence,
> temporal drift — are answerable with provenance, source roots and recorded
> time, all of which the store already has and retrieval does not use.
>
> And the benchmark was invalid twice before it measured anything. Both times
> the deliberately broken arm caught it, which is the whole argument for
> building the thing that can fail before the thing that can succeed.
